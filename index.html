01

Showing technologists what’s expected of them, and what to learn next

Engineers couldn’t easily see what was expected at their level. The answers were spread across half a dozen channels. Brief reframed after 7 learner interviews; first release planned for 18 December 2026. Read case study Ongoing McKinsey & Company 2026 Learning architecture / User research

My role

Lead designer for the technologist learning team, and leader of the squad doing the work. A senior colleague owns the product roadmap.

What I did

The request was for a “front door” website. After seven learner interviews and a benchmark, I came back with a different question, the one people actually asked: what’s expected of me at my level, and what comes next?

We narrowed the first version to software and product engineers, and to credentials and certifications. I’m leading the analysis of what people earn at each level, a profile template other roles can reuse, and a map of how learners find things today.

This is learning architecture in plain terms: deciding how a large set of learning resources fits together for one audience, so people can see a route through it.

Results

  • Pilot scope agreed with the sponsor; project plan with checkpoints through December.No learner outcomes yet. Proof of concept late November, first release 18 Dec 2026, wider launch 2027.
  • Part of a wider effort for about 3,000–4,000 engineers moving towards AI engineering.Audience size, not reach.
02

One way to read AI skills across 16+ separate initiatives

The firm ran more than 16 AI-skills initiatives and six kinds of credential, with no way to compare them. Four survey questions instead of a new survey; adopted on a small scale. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Measurement design / Stakeholder research

My role

I did the research and wrote the recommendation. Rolling it out across programmes wasn’t my decision.

What I did

I interviewed more than ten stakeholders and catalogued the data and credentials that already existed across the firm’s career milestones.

People were already tired of surveys, so I proposed adding four questions to the ones each milestone programme already runs. Three rotate and ask about using AI at work. One stays fixed and asks about discussing AI. All four measure self-efficacy, meaning how confident people feel that they can do something.

Results

  • Recommendation adopted on a small scale; I build the questions into every project I work on.Recommendation, not a firmwide rollout. I can’t see how many programmes use the questions.
03

Testing my team’s data skills before teaching them

A 31-person learning design team wanted to move from basic AI use to working with data and AI agents, without knowing its starting point. The baseline showed overconfidence on core statistics, and the session series was built around it. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Capability baseline / Data literacy

My role

Chosen by the function’s global head to lead its AI and data analytics upskilling. I designed the data-literacy part of the baseline survey and analysed the results.

What I did

The survey asked people how confident they felt, then tested them. Among those who rated their confidence at 4, about 45% answered correctly on correlation versus causation and about 56% on statistical significance.

That gap decided what we taught. I designed and ran sessions on hypothesis testing and on data analysis and visualisation, working with the firm’s analytics teams. I also co-designed the session on multi-agent systems (set-ups where several AI agents split a task between them), which became the most requested in the series, and wrote an executive overview of the topic for enterprise learning.

Results

  • 31 baseline responses; sessions delivered February to June 2026, with a monthly series continuing.Audience is peers in the learning function, not the whole firm.
  • About 19 colleagues joined the hypothesis-testing session.Attendance, from the session record.
04

Finding out whether an AI assistant could help people mid-task

A team wanted to know if an AI agent could give reliable help while people were doing their jobs, and was close to stopping the experiment. Semi-finalist in the firm’s innovation competition; the team acted on the findings about two years later. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025 AI evaluation / Research design

My role

Thought partner on the study. I worked on the agent’s instructions and co-developed the evaluation method with the people analytics team. I didn’t build or roll out the eventual solution.

What I did

I treated it as research. Before any testing, we agreed what “reliable” and “relevant” meant, then measured the agent against those definitions instead of impressions.

When the team considered quitting early, I argued for finishing, so we would have an answer either way. The framing of the problem turned out to be as useful as the result.

Results

  • Semi-finalist in the firm’s internal innovation competition, 2025.Internal recognition.
  • The team acted on the recommendations about two years later.Implementation was theirs; my part was the framing and the analysis.

05

Helping 2,200 experts get found for what they know

Most people in a 2,200-strong expert network had no published summary of their expertise, so colleagues searching for help often missed them. After the campaign, most of the experts measured moved into the top results of internal expert search. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Learning campaign / AI coach

My role

Learning designer and builder of the digital assets, in a campaign led by the network’s own learning team.

What I did

The goal was for everyone to have a value proposition: a short, evidence-backed statement of what they bring to a client. Existing programmes reached fewer than one in five people, and there were few places to practise writing one and have it challenged.

I recommended a combined approach to the network’s directors and built most of it. A mobile-friendly microsite with a building-blocks framework and interviews with colleagues who had rewritten theirs. An AI coach that asks questions instead of writing the answer for you. Materials for a ready-to-run team workshop.

For the coach, I ran user testing against a rubric, collected the feedback and got the sign-off needed to release it to the firm’s early-access users.

Results

  • Most of the experts measured moved into the top results of internal expert search, including some who had previously been hard to find at all.Internal analysis of the whole campaign, which these assets made possible; not the effect of one asset alone.
  • About 106 workshop participants given access to the coach; reused since with other teams and audiences.Access granted, not unique usage.
  • A learning team in another group asked to use the content for its own effort.Request, 2025.
06

Turning AI lectures into work on a real client case

Two AI courses, one taken across the firm and one for senior leaders, relied on lectures when people needed to work through problems. Senior participants rated their ability to reimagine a business area 2.8 before and 5.2 after, out of 7. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2026 Active learning / Squad leadership

My role

Co-led both redesigns within the squad I lead. On the firm-wide course I set the plan, then handed design ownership to a junior colleague.

What I did

I observed the firm-wide course and marked every point where learners were only listening. We rebuilt the concepts section around one client case that runs from start to finish.

For the senior programme, I co-led the redesign of an in-person workshop in Madrid and coached the same junior colleague as he built the programme’s first interactive microsite.

Results

  • Senior participants’ self-rated ability rose from 2.8 to 5.2 out of 7; relevance rated 6.0.Self-assessment, Madrid workshop, July 2026. Not a skills test.
  • On the firm-wide course, relevance rose from 5.91 to 6.3 out of 7.Survey before (n=47) and after (n=42), June 2026; shipped alongside other squad changes.
  • The microsite was showcased to the firm’s AI-for-learning community, and my colleague moved on to own a key deliverable himself.Internal community of practice.
07

Redesigning a programme only one in four eligible people used

A development programme for newly promoted professionals reached about 23% of those eligible. People found it long and repetitive. New seven-week design running now; early reports of people applying it at work. Read case study Ongoing McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Programme design / AI practice

My role

Lead designer, responsible for the structure of the programme from start to finish.

What I did

The programme builds ownership, accountability and communication before people manage others. Early on, I built a small website of the draft design with surveys embedded, so stakeholders could react before anything was final.

The result is three two-week units and a one-week capstone. Each unit mixes live virtual sessions, coaching and AI role-plays for difficult stakeholder conversations. Participants also develop their own value proposition and rehearse the firm’s communication frameworks.

Results

  • One coach reported that everyone in her groups had completed the workplace challenge: they had held the difficult conversation, or prepared and scheduled it.Self-reported, mid-programme. Not a measure of behaviour change.
  • Participants describe the content as relevant to their role, with practical takeaways.Mid-programme feedback. Final evaluation after the programme ends on 2 Dec 2026.

Theme 3

Enabling people to practise deliberately

Deliberate practice means focused, repeated practice with feedback, which is how difficult skills improve. I use AI and simulation so people can rehearse the moments that matter in their job, at a scale live coaching can’t reach.

08

Making AI feedback consistent for about 2,000 learners

An AI grader in a large firm-wide programme scored too generously while its written comments were too harsh, so learners got mixed messages. About 2,000 participants now practise against the rebuilt scoring guides. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Assessment / Applied AI

My role

I redesigned the rubrics (the scoring guides the AI uses) for written uploads and spoken role-plays.

What I did

I rewrote the criteria so the score and the comment point the same way.

Before launch, I re-ran 13 past submissions through the old and new rubrics side by side and walked stakeholders through every disagreement.

Results

  • About 2,000 participants have practised against the revised rubrics.Participants, not completions.
  • Programme owners reported more coherent feedback and more retry attempts meeting the bar.Reported by the programme; no controlled comparison.
  • Another business unit aligned its own rubrics to the new standard.2026 review.

10

Simulations that assess what people can actually do

Senior leaders needed to practise giving hard feedback, and other teams wanted to assess applied skill without a live assessor. Feedback simulation used by 1,000+ people, with difficulty tuned from pilot data. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2024–2025 Simulation / Assessment

My role

Lead designer, working with senior stakeholders, subject experts and a simulation platform.

What I did

For the feedback simulation, used by engagement leaders and team leaders, I designed the scenarios with senior stakeholders and used participant data from each pilot round to adjust how hard each one was.

For a commercial skills simulation, I storyboarded every interaction as a flow chart, so the subject expert could see the nuances and challenge them before anything was built.

I also started the first of four assessment simulations, which judge competence rather than teach. The subject experts disagreed at first, so I set up a shared visual design process to get them aligned.

Results

  • 1,000+ users of the feedback simulation.Figure from my earlier CV; difficulty tuning confirmed in the 2025 review.
  • The commercial simulation assessed applied skill in a self-paced format, which the team hadn’t attempted before.2025 review.
