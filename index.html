The destination might be clear, but the route isn’t. I collect evidence, identify gaps, and provide actionable recommendations for short-, mid-, or long-term horizons.

01

Providing engineers with clear and tailored development opportunities that move the organization forward

Situation: Engineers need to find the technical training that matters most to them, cutting through the hype and ever-increasing offerings. Complication: Information is scattered through multiple sources, some without owners, resulting in outdated information and poor relevance. New training releases are often broadcasted, targeting broad audiences even when the timing isn't right. Resolution: An architecture recommendation connecting our program sources, learning vendors, and selected people data. Read case study Ongoing McKinsey & Company 2026 Learning architecture / User research

Details

The request came in as "reimagination" of the engineers' learning journey. The stakeholders would like a central place where people could go in and obtain relevant information on their technical development.

After conducting a few user interviews, I proposed to architect a place for three users: the learning team, the engineers, and their managers. Each of them might prefer to interact with the system in different ways; a website, a dashboard, or a chatbot.

Due to the scope of the project, we agreed to start with a small subset of users to pilot, before scaling to the entire audience.

02

Creating a coherent system to detect AI skill signals and track people’s development over time

Situation: More than 16 AI-related programs and six AI credentials run across the firm, each with its own way to collect skills signals. Complication: We didn’t consistently track how skills changed over time, and weren’t sure whether programs asked about AI skills at all. Resolution: Adding targeted AI-related questions to the existing surveys that each career-milestone program already runs. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Measurement design / Stakeholder research

Details

After interviewing more than ten stakeholders who manage AI-related programs and upskilling initiatives at the firm, I mapped all skills signals we collect and highlighted the gaps and opportunities to consistently collect data so that we can track how skills develop over time.

The solution is two questions that measure self-efficacy, meaning how confident people feel they can do something: one about using AI at work, the other about discussing AI with clients.

03

Building a data and AI upskilling curriculum for the future of learning design

Situation: The learning design team needed advanced AI capabilities to shape learning in the AI era, and data and analytical skills to work in increasingly unpredictable and ambiguous situations. Complication: Given the size of the team and its wide range of expertise, we needed a reliable baseline to make sure the program would fill real gaps. Resolution: We designed and delivered monthly upskilling sessions throughout 2026, and the series is ongoing. Read case study Ongoing McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Capability baseline / Data literacy

Details

I was in charge of data analytics and the baseline survey, which I administered and analyzed. I then designed and delivered three of the monthly sessions.

The survey revealed misconceptions about how well people knew certain topics, and the sessions worked to resolve them.

04

Designing a rigorous test to determine whether an AI assistant can support people in the flow of work

Situation: A cross-functional team wanted to know whether an AI assistant could provide performance support in the flow of work and overcome the shortcomings of our legacy performance support solution. Complication: The team had a very broad research question and no clear plan to break it down into testable hypotheses. Resolution: A research experiment with a clear strategy and measurement plan that helped us improve our agentic AI skills along the way. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025 AI evaluation / Research design

Details

I was a thought partner on the study. I worked on the AI assistant’s instructions and developed the research questions, hypotheses, and the evaluation method, which I then validated with the people analytics team.

Before any testing, we agreed what “reliable” and “relevant” meant.

The study itself did not result in a replacement of our performance support platform, but it set the stage for a 2026 initiative that did.

05

Helping 2,200 expert consultants land their next engagement

Situation: Most of the 2,200 people in an expert network had no published summary of their expertise, which significantly prevented them from being staffed for engagement studies. Complication: The value of the people in this network had been increasingly and unfairly undermined by the perception that AI could replace them and their expertise. Resolution: A combined campaign of a microsite, instructor-led sessions, and an AI-based coach on value proposition to help people emphasize the value that they bring beyond output generation, particularly when combined with AI. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Learning campaign / AI coach

Details

I recommended a combined approach to the network’s directors and built most of it, working within a campaign led by the network’s own learning team.

Most experts who participated in the campaign moved into the top results of our internal search for experts. The AI-based coach has been used hundreds of times, and the value proposition learning materials have been repurposed several times for similar campaigns for other audiences.

06

Building consultative AI skills with immersive client case scenarios

Situation: As design lead, with a junior designer as my partner, I was tasked with giving new-hire consultants foundational consultative AI skills as part of their onboarding program. Complication: New hires had little to no knowledge of what their role in an AI transformation engagement would look like, and couldn’t see the use of the topics, which were delivered as a three-hour passive lecture. We couldn’t change the session length, remove topics, or change the program structure. Resolution: We turned the session into a consultative role play with a fictitious client and placed the AI concepts new hires need to own as consultants inside the scenario. Faculty and participants gave great feedback. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2026 Active learning / Squad leadership / Mentoring

Details

I worked through the design approach with my teammate, then acted as his mentor and manager so he could take ownership of the development. I set up calls with subject matter experts to ask questions and get feedback on the ideas we were brainstorming, and then he executed the design.

07

Equipping newly promoted specialists with the ownership skills they need to succeed

Situation: The firm’s people function needed to redesign a milestone program for newly promoted non-consultant specialists, equipping them with tactical, actionable steps to succeed as individual contributors. Complication: The original program had very low attendance due to its excessive length, highly demanding workload, and time commitment. It was also losing relevance because it made no mention of AI or its impact on the specialist role. Resolution: A seven-week design of three two-week units and a one-week capstone, mixing live virtual sessions, coaching, and AI role-plays for difficult stakeholder conversations. Read case study Ongoing McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Program design / AI practice

Details

I took over as project lead after a colleague interviewed talent partners and former faculty about the program’s shortcomings and opportunities. After reviewing the findings, I designed the new program’s structure, which revolves around the concept of ownership and explores it from multiple angles.

Throughout the program, I embedded virtual sessions, faculty-led coaching, and AI-based practice activities. Each session includes key concepts tailored to the specific needs of non-consultant specialists, and practical tips to embed AI in the flow of work.

Participants describe the content as relevant to their roles and as offering practical takeaways.

Enabling people to practice deliberately

Deliberate practice means focused, repeated practice with feedback, which is how difficult skills improve. I use AI and simulation so people can rehearse the moments that matter in their job, at a scale live coaching can’t reach.

08

Creating a consistent learning experience for deliberate practice in an onboarding program

Situation: We were offering AI-based deliberate practice opportunities in an onboarding program for consultants. Complication: Feedback was largely inconsistent because of the way the rubrics were written: feedback was overly harsh and grades were overly lenient. Resolution: I redesigned the rubrics around specific skills and sub-skills and tailored them to the activities learners were working on. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Assessment / Applied AI

Details

I redesigned the rubrics and tested them on earlier submissions to confirm they worked better than the originals.

Since then, more than 2,000 participants have gone through the program, and the results showed consistency and better alignment between feedback and grades.

09

Giving partners private, realistic practice for their hardest AI conversations

Situation: McKinsey partners needed to practice client conversations about AI grounded in the firm’s principles. Complication: Time constraints and the overwhelming range of possible scenarios ruled out a traditional simulation. The AI voice coach we chose instead had to ground its feedback in the firm’s consultative AI principles, rather than in traditional conversation feedback such as pace, tone of voice, and language. Resolution: A voice-based role-play coach that plays a client and gives feedback afterward, grounded in a knowledge base of the firm’s consultative AI principles. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2025–2026 Voice role-play / Executive learning

Details

I designed the AI coach and the learning pathway around it. Each version was evaluated for accuracy of content and quality of the experience, and I iterated before wider release.

Dozens of senior users have run more than 90 practice sessions. The coach was shortlisted for the firm’s internal learning award and added to its AI enablement resources.

10

Designing simulations that let people practice and prove applied skills

Situation: Senior leaders needed to practice giving hard feedback, and other teams wanted to assess applied skill without a live assessor. Complication: The team had never assessed applied skill in a self-paced format, and subject experts disagreed at first on the assessment design. Resolution: A feedback simulation used by more than 1,000 people, a self-paced commercial skills simulation that assesses applied skill, and the first of four assessment simulations. Read case study Delivered McKinsey & Company 2024–2025 Simulation / Assessment

Details

As lead designer, I worked with senior stakeholders, subject experts, and a simulation platform. For the feedback simulation, I designed the scenarios with senior stakeholders and used data from each pilot round to adjust difficulty.

For the commercial simulation, I storyboarded every interaction as a flow chart, so the subject expert could see and challenge the nuances before anything was built. For the assessment simulations, I set up a shared visual design process to align the subject experts.

“[He] knew exactly how to get the most out of the [simulation] tool and created an experience that immersed learners and allowed application-level assessment, something not previously attempted asynchronously.”
